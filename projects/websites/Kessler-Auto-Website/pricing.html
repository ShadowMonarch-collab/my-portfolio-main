What moves a price up.

Those numbers cover most cars. When yours won’t, you’ll hear it from us before we start — never as a surprise on the invoice.

Rust

This is Michigan and we salt the roads. A seized caliper bracket bolt can add an hour to an otherwise ordinary brake job. We’ll photograph it and call before that clock starts.

Size and layout

Heavy-duty trucks, large SUVs and anything where the part is buried behind other parts cost more in labor. Some engines need the intake off to reach the back plugs.

Parts choice

We default to quality aftermarket, which is what the warranty is priced around. Genuine dealer parts are available on request and cost more — sometimes a lot more, occasionally for good reason.

What we find underneath

Occasionally the failed part broke something else. That’s the one case where a price changes mid-job, and it changes only after you’ve seen a photo and said yes.

The labor rate, and what it buys.

$138 an hour, posted on the wall and on this page. Roughly the middle of the market for Grand Rapids — below the dealers, above the cheapest shops on 28th Street.

We bill actual time on most diagnostic work rather than a flat-rate book, because book time rewards rushing. On repairs we quote flat and eat the difference when a job runs long — that’s what “the quote holds” means in practice.

Our techs are salaried, not paid a commission on parts. Nobody here makes an extra dollar by finding you a problem, which is the single most important thing on this page.

Three things we’ll put in writing.

The quote holds.

The number on your report is the number you pay. If a tech opens something up and finds more, the work stops and you get a call — before the cost moves a dollar.

Three years, 36,000 miles.

Parts and labor, on every repair we do. Honored here and at roughly 1,700 shops nationwide, so a failure in Tucson isn’t your problem to argue about.

If we can’t find it, you don’t pay.

Bring us a noise or a light we can’t track down and the diagnostic is free. We’d rather eat two hours than invoice you for a guess.

Questions about money

Not as a policy, no. If you’ve got a written quote from another reputable shop that’s meaningfully below ours for the same parts and the same warranty, show us and we’ll either match it or tell you honestly to take their deal. What we won’t do is match a price that’s low because it’s using cheaper parts and calling it the same job.

Yes, with one condition: we can’t warranty the part, only our labor. If a customer-supplied part fails in six months you’ll pay the labor again, and that’s a conversation we’d rather have now than then. For a lot of jobs the maths works out fine; for brakes and anything safety-critical we’ll usually talk you out of it.

We take all major cards, and we work with a third-party financing provider for repairs over $500 — six months interest-free if you qualify. Ask at the front desk. We’ll also happily split a list into “this month” and “when you can,” and tell you honestly which items can wait.

Yes, against the first repair you approve from that visit, no minimum. Approve a $38 cabin filter and you pay $38, not $67. If you approve nothing, you pay the $29 and leave with the full report and every photograph in it — which is worth more than $29 the next time somebody quotes you.
