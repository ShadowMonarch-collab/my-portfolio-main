You’re not paranoid. The industry earned this.

About two out of three drivers say they’ve been sold a repair they didn’t need. They’re probably right. At most shops the incentives point one direction: a service writer paid on commission, a “multi-point inspection” that’s really a sales sheet, and a customer sitting in a waiting room with no way to verify a single word of it.

So you do what everyone does. You nod at the estimate, you pay it, and you drive away not knowing whether the thing you just bought was real.

We’re not going to argue you out of that. We just stopped asking you to take our word for it.

How the scan works

Three steps, and you hold the decision at every one of them.

  1. 01

    Drop it off, or wait for it.

    Ten minutes of paperwork, or none at all if you booked online. There’s coffee and decent wifi in the front room, and a window into the bay so you can watch your own car.

  2. 02

    We scan it and photograph everything.

    Forty-seven points, roughly ninety minutes, on a lift with the wheels off. Every finding gets a photo or a short video — not a checkbox. Measurements go in as numbers.

  3. 03

    You approve it line by line.

    The report hits your phone with a price beside every item. Tap what you want done. Decline the rest — including things we think are urgent. We’ll note it and move on.
