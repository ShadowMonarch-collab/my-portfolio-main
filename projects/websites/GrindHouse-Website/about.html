Nobody falls off when the whole room notices.

Grind House started with a frustration Andre Boone kept hearing: people didn't lack discipline, they lacked a place that expected them. So he built one.

Est.a warehouse on Warehouse Row

The start

Built for the person who kept restarting.

Andre spent years coaching in big-box gyms and watching good people quietly disappear. Not because they were lazy, but because nobody noticed when they stopped coming.

So he opened a room where noticing is the whole point. A cleared warehouse floor, a set of coaches who learn every name, and a membership you can walk away from at any time, which means we have to earn it every single month. The result is a gym that feels less like a facility and more like a room full of people who expect to see you.

What the room is built on.

01

The room over the individual

Willpower runs out. A crew that notices your empty spot does not. We build accountability into the room so you don't have to carry it alone.

02

Everybody scales, nobody is behind

The strongest member and the first-timer do the same workout, sized to each of them. There is no slowest person here, only your own last week.

03

A membership you can leave

No contracts, no initiation fee, no tricks to cancel. If the room stops being worth it, you walk. That keeps us honest.

04

Coaches who stay

The same faces, week after week. Continuity is what turns a workout into a relationship, and a relationship is what keeps people coming back.

Inside the house

A floor that runs on energy, not mirrors.

"We're not selling workouts. We're selling a room that expects you to walk through the door, and a group of people who are glad when you do."

Andre Boone, founder

The best way to understand it is to stand in it.
