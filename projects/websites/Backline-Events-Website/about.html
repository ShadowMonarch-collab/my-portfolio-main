About

One Too Many Toasts Ruined by a Dead Microphone.

Backline started because its founder got tired of watching good days survive on luck instead of a plan.

Founder

Dana Okafor

Ten years coordinating weddings and corporate events before starting Backline in 2018, the last four of them as a lead planner at a Portland venue that booked forty events a year.

The venue job taught Dana the real failure rate of an event day: not the big visible disasters, but the run of small ones — a late vendor, a compressed timeline, a card table missing a chair — that a good coordinator absorbs so quietly the client never learns how close the day came to going sideways.

Backline exists to make that absorption a written system instead of one person's instinct, so a client gets the same invisible save whether Dana is in the room or not.

Why the company exists

The Wedding That Started It

A 2017 reception where the venue's in-house coordinator had never run a wedding with a live band, and didn't know the mic feed needed its own circuit. It blew a breaker forty seconds into the best man's toast, and the room sat in the dark for six minutes while three people who didn't know the building looked for a panel.

The couple laughed about it eventually. Dana didn't. That fuse box should have been on a run sheet, checked at the walkthrough, with a backup circuit already identified. It wasn't a hard problem — it was an unwritten one. Backline's whole method is the answer to that one night: write the failure points down before the guests arrive, so nobody has to improvise with two hundred people watching.
