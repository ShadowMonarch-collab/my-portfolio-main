Method

The Day That Goes Right Is the Easy Document.

Every coordinator writes a timeline. We write the second one too — the one covering the day that doesn't — and we put people in the room to run it.

From Booking to Load-Out

1. Walkthrough

We stand in your venue and find the failure points specific to it: the loading door that's too narrow, the lawn that floods, the kitchen that can't plate 140 covers at once. Forty-five minutes, no deposit, and you keep the list either way.

2. The Run Book

Sixty to ninety lines, printed twice, carried by two people. Every vendor's arrival window, the name and mobile of the person driving the van, and the backup we call if that van doesn't move.

3. The Day

Two coordinators minimum, on the floor from load-in to load-out. Problems get solved by someone standing in the room where they happened, then logged, then told to you the next morning — not in the moment.

Backup for Every Failure Point

The six things that actually go wrong, in order of how often we see them. Every one has an answer written down before your event date.

Common event failure points and the backup already in place for each
Failure point What it looks like on the day The backup already in place
A vendor doesn't show The florist confirms Thursday, then stops answering at 9 a.m. Saturday. A second vendor in every category, called the week of and paid to hold the date. Swap decided by 10 a.m., not at the door.
The weather turns Rain arrives ninety minutes before an outdoor ceremony with no cover. A tent on four-hour hold, March to October. The call gets made six hours out on the forecast, not on the first drop.
The timeline slips Speeches run twenty-five minutes long and dinner service backs up behind them. Three compression points marked in the run book. The kitchen gets the call before the plates do, and dinner still lands hot.
Power or sound dies The DJ rig cuts out four bars into the first dance. A battery speaker and a spare mic in our van at every event. Music back inside four minutes, from a playlist we already hold.
Someone gets hurt or ill A guest faints during the toasts, in a room with two hundred people watching. Two coordinators first-aid certified. Venue medical contact and nearest urgent care confirmed at the walkthrough, on the run book.
A guest becomes the problem An over-served guest, or the family conflict everyone was quietly dreading. The floor captain moves it out of the room, arranges a ride, and stays with it. You hear about it tomorrow, if at all.

Questions People Ask at the Walkthrough

Do you charge for the walkthrough if we don't book?

No. The walkthrough and the failure-point list it produces are yours whether or not you hire us. Most people book after seeing the list, but that's not a condition.

How far out do you need to be brought in?

Day-of coordination starts four to six weeks out. Partial and full planning can start any time, though earlier gives more room on vendor selection and venue sourcing.

What happens if a vendor cancels the week of?

We work from a standby list of 61 vendors across the metro. A cancellation gets a replacement call the same day, and the swap is decided by mid-morning, not discovered at the door.

Do we get a copy of the run book?

Yes, before the event and again at the walkthrough for changes. It's printed twice for the day itself — one copy with the lead coordinator, one with the vendor lead.
