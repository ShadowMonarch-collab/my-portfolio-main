Most Coordinators Sell You a Timeline.

A timeline only describes the day that goes right. We write that one too, then we write the second document covering the day that doesn't, and we put people in the room to run it.

Day-of coordination

You plan it, we run it. We take over four to six weeks out, confirm every vendor, and own the day from load-in to load-out so you can actually attend your own event.

Partial planning

You have the venue and a couple of vendors. We fill the gaps, take the decisions you are tired of making, and still run the day, which means the plan and the people executing it are the same team.

Full planning

From an empty calendar to load-out. Design, budget, vendor selection and the run book, so you never have to hold two contradictory quotes and guess which one is honest.

The method

The Run Book

Sixty to ninety lines, printed twice, carried by two people. Every vendor's arrival window, the name and mobile of the person actually driving the van, and the backup we call if that van doesn't move.

It means a florist who cancels at 9 a.m. costs you a phone call you never hear about, instead of the centrepieces.

A Team on Site, Not a Number to Call.

Two coordinators minimum, three past 120 guests, on the floor the whole time. Nobody is running your day from a laptop across town, so a problem gets solved by someone standing in the room where it happened.

Crises You Never Knew About

Two events where something went badly wrong. In both, the couple found out afterwards.

11:00 a.m. · 90 minutes to the ceremony

Rain, and a lawn ceremony with no cover.

The forecast turned at nine. We released the tent hold at eleven rather than waiting for rain, and the crew had it up by half past twelve. The ceremony started on schedule, dry, in front of 96 guests.

“My mother still thinks we planned the tent all along. Nobody asked me a single question that morning.”

Priya C., wedding, Sauvie Island

9:48 p.m. · four bars into the first dance

The DJ’s rig died in front of everyone.

A blown amp, mid-song. The battery speaker came out of our van and the track restarted from our own copy of the playlist. Three minutes and forty seconds of quiet, and most of the room read it as a pause in the song.

“Our photographer told us what happened a week later. We genuinely had not noticed.”

Owen M., wedding, Portland
